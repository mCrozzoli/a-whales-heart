About

The Piece

A Whale's Heart is a data-driven sound installation that sonifies the diving behaviour of a whale using real biologging data. The depth of the whale drives all sonic parameters: amplitude, heart rate, and pitch. The piece explores fragility, scale, and the physiology of the largest animal on Earth.

As the whale descends, its heart slows to as few as 2 beats per minute, the sound fades to near silence, and the pitch drops. At the surface the heart races, the beat is loud and bright. You are listening to 27 hours of a whale's life.

Playback

The dataset spans 23 hours 34 minutes of continuous diving recorded at 1 Hz (one depth sample per second), subsampled to every 10 seconds for sonification (8,488 frames).

Heart Rate Science

Based on Goldbogen et al. (2019), who attached an ECG recorder to a free-diving blue whale in Monterey Bay — the first direct measurement of heart rate in the world's largest animal.

This installation maps depth to beat interval (time between beats), not BPM directly. The silence between beats grows proportionally with depth — which is what a listener perceives. The aortic arch acts as a hydraulic buffer between the slow, irregular heartbeats.

Goldbogen, J.A. et al. (2019). Extreme bradycardia and tachycardia in the world's largest animal. PNAS, 116(50), 25329-25332.

Feeding Lunges

Blue whales are lunge feeders — they accelerate into dense patches of krill with their mouths open, engulfing up to 80,000 litres of water in a single gulp. Each lunge requires enormous cardiac output.

Detection heuristic: depth > 65m and ascending > 2m per 10-second sample. When a lunge is detected, the heart rate spikes ×2.5 (capped at 75 bpm). Amplitude is deliberately unchanged — the energetic burst of feeding is felt as rhythm acceleration only, not volume change.

Data Sources

Sound layer — Icelandic whale, 1 Hz depth time series, June 28-29 2018 (27 hours, 97,321 samples). Maximum depth 133.4m. Source: IcelandData/whale_depth.xlsx.

Spatial layer — Pacific blue whale GPS track (2015CA-MK10-00838, female, ~70ft, Point Mugu CA, July-August 2015, 516 fixes after cleaning). Temporary placeholder until Iceland GPS data is obtained. Drives stereo L/R panning.

Blue and Fin Whale Movements, California 2014-2017 + Oregon 2018. DOI: 10.24431/rw1k463. PI: Daniel Palacios + Bruce Mate, Marine Mammal Institute, Oregon State University.
